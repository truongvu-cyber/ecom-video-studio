# Viral playbook — how short-form ad creators actually think

Read this before Stage 2 (direction and story draft). It is the reasoning
behind the story table, not a template. Everything here is about one number:
**how many people are still watching at second N**. Every decision is judged
by whether it keeps the thumb from scrolling.

## 1. The retention curve is the whole game

Short-form platforms rank a video by watch time and completion, so:

- **0–1.5 s decides everything.** Most drop-off happens before the first
  cut. Frame 1 must already contain the interesting thing: the product doing
  its trick, a face mid-reaction, a visual contradiction. Never open on an
  establishing shot, a logo, a black frame, a hand reaching for something.
- **Result first, process second.** Show the glowing logo on the ground, then
  the install. Show the name on the hoodie, then the typing. The viewer must
  know what they are waiting for before they are asked to wait.
- **Re-hook every 2–4 s.** Each cut, text slam or camera change is a small
  new promise. A shot that adds no new information (same subject, same angle,
  same idea) is where people leave.
- **Hold the payoff, rush the setup.** The install steps run at 1.2–1.6 s
  each; the reveal gets 2–2.5 s. Pacing is uneven on purpose.
- **End mid-energy or on a loop.** A last frame that resembles the first
  frame makes replays feel seamless; replays count as watch time.

## 2. Hook types that work for products (pick by what frame 1 can show)

| type | frame 1 shows | text / line | use when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Result-first | the trick happening | "Open the door…" / "Wait for it" | the product has a visible effect (light, print, transformation) |
| POV | the viewer's own hands or view | "POV: his gift has his name on it" | gifts, personal items, daily-use products |
| Contradiction | something that should not work | "It's $12 and it does *this*" (only with a verified price) | clever/cheap gadgets |
| Call-out | a face looking at the lens | "Milwaukee fans, look at this." | fandom, niche identity products |
| Problem | the annoyance, visible | "Gifts for him always end up in a drawer" | gift products |
| Process tease | hands mid-action, result hidden | "I put his name on it…" | personalization, DIY |
| Social proof | the reaction of someone else | "He wore it three days straight" (only if true) | gifts, apparel |

Rules: the hook is **concrete** (a thing you can see or count), **short**
(≤ 7 words spoken, ≤ 4 words on screen), and **matches frame 1**. A hook
line about something not yet visible is a broken promise.

## 3. One idea per video

A video sells one thing: *the name goes on it*, *the logo appears when you
open the door*, *the print is gold*. Features that do not serve that idea go
in another video. If the story table needs two ideas, make two videos; they
cost the same and test twice as much.

## 4. Text on screen

- One slam = one idea, **≤ 4 words**, biggest word is the keyword.
- Text carries the **claim**, the picture carries the **proof**. Do not
  caption what is obviously visible ("a hoodie"); do caption what the eye
  cannot know ("any name", "no tools", "60 seconds").
- Reading time ≈ 0.3 s per word + 0.5 s; a 4-word slam needs ≥ 1.7 s on screen.
- Numbered steps (1. 2. 3. 4.) are a retention device: people stay to see
  the sequence finish. Keep the count ≤ 5.
- Everything must work **muted** (a large share watches without sound) and
  must not depend on text alone (another share watches with sound and
  half-attention): text and sound say the same thing in different ways.

## 5. Sound

- Beat-synced cuts read as "professional" even in phone footage; it is the
  cheapest quality signal there is.
- The drop is a resource: spend it on the payoff shot (the reveal, the
  celebration), not on the CTA.
- Hype formats: cut SFX (whoosh before, impact on the cut) on every hard
  cut; a riser into the drop.
- Dialogue formats: the voice is the hook; music sits well under it.
- Trending sounds help discovery only when the edit actually follows their
  rhythm; a trending sound on an unrelated edit does nothing.

## 6. Pacing by format

| format | average shot | rhythm |
| --- | --- | --- |
| hype montage | 0.8–1.6 s | 3 fast cuts → 1 longer hold (the payoff), repeat |
| UGC talking | 2.5–4 s | cut on sentence ends, punch-in instead of a new angle |
| demo / how-to | 1.2–2 s per step | steps fast, result slow |
| ASMR / satisfying | 4–8 s | few cuts, let the texture play |

Vary the rhythm. Eight identical 2-second shots feel like a slideshow.

## 7. CTA

- Soft beats hard: "link below", "grab yours", "comment TEAM for the link"
  outperform "BUY NOW 50% OFF".
- CTA appears **with the product in frame**, during the last 2–3 s, never on
  an empty end card.
- A comment bait ("comment your team") doubles as a ranking signal.
- Never a CTA for a claim the product file marks unknown (no price, no
  shipping promise).

## 8. Native feel beats polish

Phone framing, handheld micro-shake, real rooms, imperfect light. Over-clean
AI footage reads as an ad and gets skipped; the prompts therefore ask for
"phone footage", "no color grading", lived-in backgrounds. The edit may add
energy (flash, punch, text) but should not add gloss (no cinematic LUTs).

## 9. Proof hierarchy

Demonstration (the thing happening on camera) > comparison (before/after,
with/without) > social proof (reaction, review — only real ones) > claim
(text alone). Climb as high as the product allows; a video that only claims
is the weakest possible video.

## 10. Make three, not one

The hook decides most of the result and nobody can predict which hook wins.
Every project ships **3 hook variants** (cheap: overlay text and, if
dialogue, the first line) on the same footage. Watch the 3-second hold rate
and completion per variant; keep the winner's pattern for the next product.

## Draft checklist (run it on the story table before asking for approval)

1. **Thumb test** — does frame 1, on its own, make someone stop? What exactly is moving or surprising?
2. **New-info test** — does every shot add something the previous one did not?
3. **Payoff test** — is there one moment worth screenshotting? Is it held long enough?
4. **One-idea test** — can the video be summarized in one sentence?
5. **Muted test** — does it make sense with the sound off? With the screen half-covered by UI?
6. **Truth test** — is every claim on screen backed by `product.json` facts?
7. **Rhythm test** — is there at least one tempo change?
8. **Loop test** — does the last shot flow back into the first?
9. **Variant test** — are there 3 distinct hooks (different *type*, not just wording)?
